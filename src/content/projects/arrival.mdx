## Why two pages

*Arrival* and *Story of Your Life* are the same story told so differently that
one page cannot serve both. The film is something you have already seen and are
trying to understand — the second viewing is where it opens up, and an explainer
for it can safely say everything. The novella is something you are in the middle
of reading, where saying everything is the one thing that ruins it.

So: two pages, deliberately separate, linked to each other with the direction of
travel marked. Going from the book reference to the film explainer carries a
**含剧透** flag. Going the other way carries a **不剧透** one.

## The film explainer

Built for the rewatch, which is when *Arrival* stops being a puzzle and starts
being a decision.

- **The real timeline.** The film's central trick is that the daughter scenes are
  shot like memory and are actually the future. A toggle reorders the same events
  between 银幕顺序 and 真实顺序, so you can see the edit doing the deceiving.
- **A circular-writing sandbox.** Heptapod B writes a whole sentence as one ring,
  which means the writer must know the entire sentence before the first stroke.
  Click the components in any order and the ring assembles identically — the point
  being that there is no order to get wrong.
- **What the film changed.** A side-by-side against the novella: the 112 remote
  "looking glasses" became twelve ships you walk into, the entire global-crisis
  thread is original to the film, the daughter dies at twenty-five of a climbing
  accident rather than young of illness, and the physicist is renamed from 加里 to
  伊恩.

The two images are original atmospheric illustrations, not film stills, and the
page says so in the alt text and the footer.

## The book reference

This one is generated, not written. It comes out of
[the EPUB reading-guide tool](/projects/odyssey-reader/) I built for exactly this
problem: translated novels render names phonetically, and 露易丝·班克斯 /
韦伯上校 / 弗莱帕 / 拉斯伯里 blur together in a way the originals never do.

Fifteen characters and twenty-three terms, each tagged with the section it first
appears in, behind a progress slider. Set it to 0 — before you have opened the
book — and the page shows nothing at all.

Two things the extraction got right that I want to record, because they are the
difference between a useful tool and a plausible one. It gave the unnamed "you"
the story is addressed to its own entry, which is the whole shape of the novella.
And it declined to connect 你父亲 to any named character, because the text in
those sections never says who he is — the tool does not infer relationships the
prose has not stated, which is precisely what keeps it safe to read early.

Fermat's principle, 变分原理, 述行语词, 七肢桶语言B — the glossary turned out to
matter as much as the names. The physics is not decoration in this story; it is
the same idea as the language, viewed from the other side.
